# The problem statement, explained

Source: official brief, `00_brief/DICE_S3_case_brief_C2M.pdf` (Meesho DICE Challenge Season 3, Business Track).

## In one line

Meesho wants more factories selling directly on its platform, so prices drop for real, and it wants them to stay. The brief wants a plan that does both without paying sellers to stay and without hiring an army of people.

## 1. Why prices are higher than they need to be

A typical Indian product passes through several hands before an online buyer sees it:

**Factory → distributor → wholesaler → reseller → Meesho listing → buyer**

Each middle layer adds its own margin, so the buyer pays more. Cutting out the middle layers is the obvious fix: the factory sells directly to the buyer through Meesho. That is **C2M (Consumer-to-Manufacturer)**: Meesho sourcing directly from manufacturers instead of resellers.

## 2. Why Meesho cares

Meesho's promise to buyers is **the lowest price**. The brief says C2M "is not a nice to have category play, it is a direct lever on the platform's central value proposition."

The brief separates two kinds of low price:

| | Discounted price | Structurally lower price |
|---|---|---|
| Why it is cheap | Someone pays for the discount | It genuinely costs less to make and sell |
| Lasts? | Stops when the money stops | Permanent |
| Rivals can copy? | Yes, with a bigger budget | No, it comes from how the factory works |

Meesho wants the second kind: manufacturers who **own a meaningful part of their value chain** (raw material, production, scale or automation).

## 3. The hidden trap

> "Seller scale is often used as a starting signal for who might have this advantage, but scale alone does not always translate into a lower price on the platform."

**Big does not mean cheap.** A seller with ₹50 crore of sales might be a large trader reselling someone else's goods; a smaller factory that spins its own yarn might be far cheaper. Picking manufacturers by size picks the wrong ones. This is why our solution screens sellers on actual prices buyers paid, not turnover (the Price Truth Index).

## 4. The objective: two problems, not one

> "Design a strategy to meaningfully grow Meesho's C2M seller base and their long-term contribution to price competitiveness."

- **Problem 1, they don't come.** Large manufacturers run bulk, made-to-order B2B with no returns. Selling one unit at a time means new operations, inventory risk and a real cost if it fails.
- **Problem 2, they don't stay.** Early orders are weak, confidence falls, and they exit "before the channel becomes meaningful for them."

The brief warns that solving one side fails: attracting factories with no plan to keep them, or keeping a few through effort that can't be repeated at scale.

## 5. The four challenges, from the factory owner's side

| Challenge | What the owner is thinking | What a solution must do |
|---|---|---|
| Operational hassle | "I ship trucks, not parcels. I don't want to learn single-order packing or returns." | Take unit-level work off their hands |
| Inventory risk | "In B2B nothing is made until it's sold. Here you want me to stock up and hope?" | Keep the made-to-order model |
| Unproven demand | "Will there be enough orders?" The brief: "An unquantified promise isn't enough." | Show real numbers before they commit |
| Weak early scale-up | New sellers get only a small share of orders in their first 30 days | Get them early orders without paying for them |

## 6. Who benefits

- **Manufacturers:** a new channel and better use of idle capacity, **without abandoning B2B**. C2M is an extra channel.
- **Buyers:** genuinely lower prices where a real cost advantage exists, not discounts.
- **Meesho:** a lasting price advantage that **"compounds over time"** (why judges score "10x and long-term thinking").

## 7. The four questions the strategy must answer

1. Which manufacturer segments and categories have a genuine price advantage, and which don't despite scale?
2. What stops reluctant manufacturers, and what would credibly change their calculus?
3. Once onboarded, what makes early scale-up sustainable **without subsidies or manual handholding** that can't scale to hundreds of sellers?
4. What should Meesho measure to know a seller is on track, and when to intervene?

Two constraints run through all four: **no unsustainable subsidy** and **no manpower that can't scale**.

## 8. Round 2 deliverable (6–10 slide deck)

1. Manufacturer segmentation by cohort: offline / online-active-elsewhere / previously churned from Meesho
2. Market sizing and the price/NMV impact of closing the gap
3. Onboarding model: support stack, risk-sharing, assisted operations
4. Sustainable scale-up model
5. Metrics and KPIs across onboarding, activation, retention
6. 30-60-90 day roadmap
7. Risks and guardrails

Plus a **working prototype** (stage page). Scoring: research quality, depth of analysis, innovation, 10x and long-term thinking, feasibility, presentation.

## 9. How our solution maps to it

| The brief's problem | Our answer |
|---|---|
| Big ≠ cheap | Price Truth Index: badge only if most products are at least 8% cheaper than market |
| Operational hassle | Factory Node: one bulk drop; the node packs, ships, handles returns |
| Inventory risk | Pre-order batches: make confirmed orders plus a 15% buffer |
| Unproven demand | Demand Brief: real weekly orders by district before committing |
| Weak first 30 days | Free extra visibility (impression grants) when orders lag, instead of cash |
| No handholding | Health Score + automatic rules; a person only when a fix fails |
| Long term | Graduated factories become Meesho Mall value brands |

## Glossary

- **C2M:** Consumer-to-Manufacturer (also written Customer-to-Manufacturer): buyers purchase directly from the factory, no intermediaries. The brief uses "Consumer-to-Manufacturer".
- **NMV:** net merchandise value, Meesho's sales after cancellations and returns.
- **AOV:** average order value (₹265 in FY26).
- **RTO:** return to origin, an order that is never delivered and comes back.
- **Cohort A / B / C:** offline-only B2B / online elsewhere / churned from Meesho.
