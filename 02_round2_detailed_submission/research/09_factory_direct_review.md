# Review: "Meesho Factory Direct" proposal

Reviewed 1 Oct 2026. An external write-up proposing one integrated system, "Meesho Factory Direct": a demand-led C2M operating layer that lets manufacturers sell B2C without becoming B2C operators.

## What it says

1. **Flip the factory's journey.** Today: make stock → list → hope → ship each order → absorb returns. Proposed: Meesho finds demand → checks the cost advantage → sends a production brief → small confirmed batch → bulk dispatch to a node → Meesho runs B2C → data comes back → winners scale.
2. **Five engines:** Price Truth, Demand, Batch, Fulfilment & Trust, Growth (Health Score decides scale up vs diagnose/exit).
3. **Graduation ladder by batch size:** Test (100–500 units) → Prove (500–2,000) → Scale (2,000–10,000+) → Strategic (forecasting, faster settlement, working capital, dedicated capacity).
4. **Financing only after proof:** no upfront cash for everyone (moral hazard); batch financing after successful, low-return batches.
5. **Meesho pays for infrastructure and data, not subsidies;** it earns through more orders and ads, not fees.
6. **Five-step admission gate:** cost advantage, category suitability, quality, capacity, price reaching the consumer.
7. **North star "C2M Value Created"** = consumer savings + manufacturer contribution + Meesho contribution.
8. **Pitch:** "Don't onboard manufacturers. Onboard manufacturing capability." To factories: "Tell us what you can make; we'll tell you what India wants you to make."

## Verdict

About 80% overlaps with our solution (Price Truth Index, Demand Brief, pre-order batches, Factory Node, Health Score, graduation, gated spend, 30-60-90 with a control group). **It is not a different solution; it is a better story for ours.**

## Statistics it cites: checked

- **ICRIER 2025 survey: verified.** See `10_facts_and_sources.md` for the full list of exit reasons. The proposal omitted competition (40%), staff (33%) and capacity (30%).
- **Meesho Q1 FY27 numbers: verified.** 274 mn ATU (+29%), 1.04 mn annual transacting sellers (+81%). Our deck still uses FY26 (264 mn, 9.6 lakh).
- **Not yet verified:** that Meesho already compensates suppliers for wrong returns automatically using package images; that the IPO prospectus shows Meesho's seller cost below rivals (Redseer). Check before use.

## Better than our deck: adopt

| Idea | Why |
|---|---|
| One named system + one-line pitch | Easier for judges to remember than many levers |
| Quality and capacity checks in the admission gate | We test only price and category |
| Graduation ladder by batch size | Clearer than a single "score 80 for 30 days" rule |
| Returns inspected and graded at the node; only defects go back | More concrete than "returns end at the node" |
| Three-party north star | Shows all sides must win |

## Weaker or risky: keep ours

1. **No answer to the weak first 30 days.** "Controlled pilot batches" don't create orders. Keep visibility grants (the CEO's own diagnosis is a visibility problem).
2. **"Meesho decides what to make, the price, and runs the stock" reads as inventory control** (Press Note 2). Keep: seller owns the stock and sets the final price; Meesho sells services at arm's length.
3. **Misreads our ₹22 cr** as subsidies; it is the Year-1 programme cost (onboarding, briefs, node, returns pool).
4. **No numbers.** The brief requires sizing and price/NMV impact; our deck has them.
5. **Its north star can't be measured directly** (Meesho can't see a factory's true profit); needs a proxy such as price kept below market plus repeat batches.

## Recommended changes to the deck

Adopt the Factory Direct framing and pitch; add quality and capacity gates, the batch-size graduation ladder and returns grading; add ICRIER statistics to Segmentation and Q1 FY27 numbers to the KPI strip. Keep visibility grants, the sizing numbers, compliance safeguards and the Day-30 test.
