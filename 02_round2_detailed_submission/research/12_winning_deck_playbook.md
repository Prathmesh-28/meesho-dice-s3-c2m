## Part 1: What the winners actually do (12 patterns)

| # | Pattern | Where it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Answer first.** One page states the recommendation and its logic before any analysis. | War Room slide 2: executive summary drawn as a logic map (transport type → challenges and trends → recommendations). Compendium: "treat the deck as one large pyramid, with the main takeaway on the title slide"; an executive summary is "not an index — each point should convey the essence". |
| 2 | **Research made visible, with n and names.** | WiRED: "08 personal interviews · 03 cities · 18–35 yrs" as big numbers, plus verbatim quotes with name, age and city. LIME: n = 79 with age and income split, survey link in the appendix. War Room: methodology slide (10+ databases, named officials interviewed, 280 approached / 174 eligible survey) and field photos of an e-rickshaw driver with his quote and daily earnings. Abbott: n = 122 as big-number tiles ("40%", "5 in 8", "57 of 120"). |
| 3 | **Problem → named solutions.** Pain points map one-to-one onto solutions with memorable names. | WiRED: pain points → categories → *UnKart-It*, *Flip-n-Fit*, *Smart Buy*. Abbott: own framework *AFA* (Awareness–Functionality–Assurance). War Room: "Mahindra: the *Apple of Mobility*". |
| 4 | **Each solution shown as mechanism + mock-up + impact.** | WiRED: step-flow, numbered app screens, impact boxes per feature. Abbott: packaging with cost per unit, website, WhatsApp flow, billboard ads. LIME: finished print ads. |
| 5 | **Every chart carries a takeaway box.** | Abbott: "Key takeaways" beside each chart. LIME: insight box under every survey chart. War Room: "Key takeaways" row under the Harvey-ball table. |
| 6 | **Numbers come with their assumptions.** | WiRED: cost table where every line has a source/assumption column, plus the Excel model embedded. Abbott: "Assumptions" and "Approach" panels beside every financial table; customer-acquisition cost per channel with cited conversion rates. Compendium market-size review: TAM = formula, then "Key assumption 1…4" listed separately. |
| 7 | **Competitors benchmarked with real logos and numbers.** | WiRED: Amazon vs Flipkart engagement table with winners marked. LIME: social-media interactivity scatter of banks, with data table and source. Abbott: positioning map built from brand logos. Compendium: "3–5 competitors × 4–5 parameters, quantitative not only qualitative". |
| 8 | **Implementation as a visual roadmap.** | Abbott: a road with month milestones. Compendium praises a phased (Phase 1–5) roadmap covering research, product, finance and timeline. |
| 9 | **Navigation and action titles.** | War Room: tab bar across the top; two-part titles ("e-Rickshaws: next big disruptive innovation in Tier 2–3 mass transport"). Compendium: "a clear headline that tells the story"; "title gives a clear glimpse, unlike a 1–2 word title". |
| 10 | **The appendix carries the depth.** | LIME: 7 of 20 slides are appendix (full survey charts, competitor tables). War Room: appendix with definitions and cost tables. WiRED: "refer to exhibits" and an embedded spreadsheet. |
| 11 | **Visual balance and discipline.** | Mix of icons, real photos and logos; one palette; small font range; whitespace; no full stops in bullets; spell out abbreviations; **avoid vertical text** (all from compendium reviews). |
| 12 | **Two kinds of judges.** | Compendium: first-cut screeners (organisers) reward "clean visuals and formatting more than deep strategic text"; final judges (senior company experts) test research depth, innovation and Q&A — "be prepared to defend every part of your solution". |

**What loses** (compendium "common pitfalls" and reviews): generic ideas and text that read as AI-written, unvalidated data, text-heavy slides, inconsistent formatting, frameworks with no insight, ignoring risks and execution, repetitive filler (the same point in three places), icons that don't match the message.

## Part 3: How to build it (process for the last three days)

1. **Ghost deck first (titles only).** Write the 10 action titles below on blank slides. Read them in order: they must tell the whole argument. Fix the story before touching layout.
2. **Mother doc.** `research/10_facts_and_sources.md` is the single source of truth for every number; anything not there is either sourced now or labelled an assumption.
3. **One hero exhibit per slide**, plus a takeaway box. Decide now what moves to the appendix.
4. **Fill the evidence gaps fast** (these are what the winners had and we don't):
   - 3–4 real manufacturer quotes from the Round 1 interviews (name or initials, cluster, firm size).
   - Photos from cluster visits, if any exist.
   - A 10-question Google Form to 30–50 buyers who use Meesho (waiting 7 days for ₹20–40 off; trust in "factory direct"). One evening is enough for n ≈ 30–50.
5. **Design system for the 20 × 11.25 in template:**
   - Title 28–32 pt bold, as an action statement; panel headers 16–18 pt; body 14–15 pt; sources 11–12 pt.
   - At these sizes a slide holds roughly 350–450 words. That is the budget, which forces the cuts.
   - Palette: Meesho plum, one orange accent, neutrals; green/amber/red only for status.
   - One icon family; real logos only for competitor comparisons; photos for people.
   - Remove the vertical rail text; keep the top tab bar.
   - No full stops in bullets; spell out NMV, AOV and RTO once.
6. **Build each slide:** title, hero exhibit, takeaway box, source line.
7. **Appendix or backup PDF:** sizing workings, NPV and sensitivity, survey charts, interview list, threshold rationale, compliance detail, glossary, sources.
8. **QA:** the title test (titles alone tell the story); the 20-second test per slide; every number checked against the fact library; "so what?" test per panel; one font family, small size range; PDF exported from PowerPoint.
9. **Q&A prep:** a sheet of the 15 hardest questions (₹1.43 cr yield, comparable products and quality, how Meesho earns from C2M, Press Note 2, cannibalisation, why 8%, pre-order uptake) each tied to a backup slide.

## One caution from the compendium

Judges and screeners flag "generic ideas from AI" and text that "seems AI-generated" as a common reason decks lose; winners stress ideas they developed themselves, backed by their own field data. The strongest defence is what only your team has: real interview quotes, cluster photos, your own survey numbers, and wording in your own voice. Rewrite any slide text that sounds generic before submitting.
